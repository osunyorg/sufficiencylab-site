The
doctoral project will be carried out under the co-supervision of Yamina Saheb,
WSL's Executive Director, and Sihem Jouini, Associate Professor at HEC Paris,
Director of the Innovation and design Project Major and the Executive MBA Major
"Innovate as an Entrepreneur"

Project
context

The 3S
project ( Systèmes de Santé Sobres ) is a flagship initiative within the
World Sufficiency Lab's strategy to co-produce actionable scientific knowledge
with societal stakeholders on critical sufficiency policy challenges.

The
doctoral candidate will design and implement an innovative action-research
programme in partnership with actors across the French health system, with the
objective of supporting the metamorphosis of healthcare delivery and governance
models to meet emerging health needs under conditions of demographic ageing,
ecological degradation, and resource constraints.

To our
knowledge, no current research initiative is specifically dedicated to
anticipating and preparing the French health system for the profound systemic
transformations that these intersecting challenges are expected to generate.

Combining
insights from public health, sustainability science, economics, and systems
analysis, the project will employ a robust mixed-methods approach, including
systematic literature reviews, systems modelling, focus groups, and machine
learning techniques. Working closely with sector stakeholders, it will
co-produce evidence-based policy pathways and governance innovations to enhance
the resilience, sustainability, and adaptive capacity of the French health
system.  

Research
objectives The primary
objective of this research is to conduct a forward-looking and participatory
inquiry aimed at co-identifying, alongside relevant stakeholders, the
transformations required for the French health system to meet future population
health needs in the context of demographic ageing and accelerating ecological
pressures.

More
specifically, the doctoral candidate will investigate the organisational,
economic, governance and business models capable of ensuring equitable access to
high-quality healthcare while strengthening the long-term sustainability and
resilience of the health system. Particular attention will be paid to the
financing mechanisms, institutional arrangements, workforce requirements, and
socio-economic and environmental implications associated with these models.

The
research will seek to identify
Organisational and
service-delivery models capable of guaranteeing universal access to
high-quality healthcare for all while remaining within, or minimising
pressures on, planetary boundaries.
The financing mechanisms,
governance arrangements, and health workforce competencies required to
support the implementation and scaling of these models.
The socio-economic and
environmental impacts of selected models, assessed through quantitative
and systems-based modelling approaches.
Evidence-based public policy
recommendations capable of facilitating the transition towards more
resilient, equitable, and sustainable health systems.

Research
questions

The
research will address the following overarching questions


How must the French health
system evolve to protect and promote population health, particularly that
of older adults, in a context characterised by digital transformation,
demographic ageing, and escalating environmental disruption?
What contribution can
sufficiency-oriented approaches make to enhancing the resilience,
sustainability, and adaptive capacity of the French health system?
What roles might existing
health system actors play in future healthcare systems, and how could
their organisational and economic models evolve in response to emerging
demographic, environmental, and technological challenges?
Which policy, governance, and
financing mechanisms are required to support the transition towards
sufficiency-oriented health-system models in France and, more broadly,
across Europe?

Methodology This
doctoral project adopts a multidisciplinary and mixed-methods approach,
combining quantitative, qualitative, and computational research methods to
investigate pathways towards a more sustainable, resilient, and equitable
French health system.

The
research will begin with a systematic literature review examining the
intersections between health systems, sustainability transitions, resilience,
and sufficiency. This review will establish the project's conceptual and
empirical foundations and identify relevant international experiences and
emerging policy innovations.

Building on
this evidence base, the project will employ systems-based modelling
approaches to assess the socio-economic, health, and environmental
implications of selected organisational and service-delivery models. These
analyses will enable the evaluation of potential trade-offs, synergies, and
long-term impacts associated with alternative health-system pathways.

The
quantitative component will be complemented by an extensive programme of stakeholder
engagement , including focus groups, semi-structured workshops, and
participatory foresight exercises involving key actors across the French health
system, such as mutual insurance organisations ( mutuelles ), public
authorities, healthcare professionals, civil society organisations, and elected
representatives.

These
activities will support the co-production of knowledge and the identification
of feasible and desirable transition pathways. The project will also leverage machine-learning
and artificial intelligence tools to support evidence synthesis, policy
mapping, and stakeholder analysis, enabling the systematic processing of large
bodies of textual and documentary data.

In parallel to the doctoral research, the Francophone Assises of
Sufficiency will be convened to bring together researchers, policymakers,
practitioners, and civil-society actors from France, Belgium, Luxembourg, and
Switzerland. These Assises will serve as a platform for knowledge exchange,
agenda-setting, and stakeholder mobilisation around key sufficiency challenges,
with particular attention to the future of health systems. Their outcomes will
contribute to refining the research agenda and strengthening the project's
transnational network of partners and collaborators. 

Target
audiences and intended impact

The
research is designed to engage a broad range of stakeholders involved in
shaping the future of the French health system. Its primary audiences include
senior officials within national and regional administrations, particularly the
Ministries of Health, Labour, Social Affairs, Ecological Transition, and
Finance; mutual insurance organisations ( mutuelles ); healthcare
providers and professional associations; elected representatives at both
national and local levels; and civil-society organisations active in the fields
of health and social protection.

By
generating robust evidence and fostering stakeholder participation throughout
the research process, the project seeks to support strategic decision-making on
the future of healthcare provision in a context of demographic, environmental,
and economic change. Its ultimate objective is to contribute to the
metamorphosis towards a more resilient, equitable, and sustainable health
system capable of ensuring universal access to prevention, care, and long-term
support while responding effectively to the challenges of the twenty-first
century.

Beyond its
academic contribution, the project aims to strengthen dialogue between
researchers, policymakers, practitioners, and citizens, thereby enhancing the
capacity of health system actors to anticipate and manage systemic
transformations.  

Deliverables
and timeline

The
doctoral project will be conducted over a three-year period and will generate a
range of academic and policy-oriented outputs.

The expected deliverables include:


An initial progress report
submitted six months after the start of the doctoral programme, presenting
the conceptual framework, literature review, and detailed research design.
A comprehensive progress report
at the end of the second year, summarising preliminary findings and
stakeholder engagement activities.
Three peer-reviewed scientific
articles submitted to leading international journals in the fields of
health policy, sustainability transitions, public administration, or
related disciplines.
Policy briefs and
stakeholder-oriented outputs designed to support knowledge transfer and
inform public debate.
A doctoral dissertation
submitted at the end of the third year, ideally structured as a thesis by
publication integrating the three scientific articles within a coherent
analytical framework.

Governance:

The project
will be supported by a dedicated governance structure designed to ensure
scientific excellence, stakeholder engagement, and policy relevance throughout
the doctoral programme.

A steering
committee composed of funding organisations, institutional partners, and
academic supervisors will meet every four months to review progress, provide
strategic guidance, and facilitate the translation of research findings into
policy and practice. This forum will ensure that the project's research agenda
remains responsive to emerging challenges and stakeholder needs while
preserving its scientific independence.

Regular
co-production workshops and structured exchanges between the doctoral
candidate, project partners, and funders will be organised throughout the
research process. These interactions will facilitate knowledge sharing,
support the refinement of research questions and analytical frameworks, and
promote the dissemination and uptake of findings among key health-system
stakeholders.

The
governance model is intended to foster a continuous dialogue between science,
policy, and practice, thereby maximising both the scientific contribution and
the societal impact of the research.

Funding
and conditions The World Sufficiency Lab (FSL) has already secured core funding for the project through
its partnership with actors from the insurance sector. Additional funding will
be sought through the French National Association for Research and Technology
(ANRT) under the CIFRE scheme following the selection of the doctoral
candidate.

Duration:

The
doctoral project will be conducted over a period of three years. The successful
candidate is expected to start the programme no later than November 2026, with
completion and submission of the doctoral dissertation no later than 31
December 2029.  

Location: 

The
doctoral candidate will be based in Paris and integrated into the activities of
FSL and HEC. The position will involve regular travel within France and, where
relevant, across Europe to support fieldwork activities, stakeholder
engagement, dissemination events, and meetings of the project's governance
bodies.  

All
activities are conducted in accordance with FSL's Code of Conduct, Ethics
Framework, and Safeguarding Policy.
