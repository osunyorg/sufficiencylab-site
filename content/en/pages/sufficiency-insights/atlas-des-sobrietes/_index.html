Text version of the Sufficiency Atlas (Atlas des Sobriétés)

This text gives the main content of the interactive map above, for screen-reader users, slow connections or browsers without JavaScript. Figures as of 21 November 2026. Open the Atlas full screen: https://atlas.apps.thesufficiencylab.org/?lang=en

What the Atlas shows

Two diagnoses for each territory in France:

1. What already exists and is not used: vacant and under-occupied dwellings, empty premises, brownfields, land.

2. Whether people can live decently in our territories: cold, cramped space, access to services and green space, housing cost burden.

Key figures for France

- 1,493,721 dwellings vacant for 2 years or more.

- 13.9 million under-occupied main residences.

- 9.5% of main residences are overcrowded.

- 13.4% of dwellings are estimated to be rated F or G (whole stock, SDES scale at 1 January 2025).

- 30.9% of households live within a 300 m walk of a public green space of at least 0.5 ha (WHO).

- 26% of households have the 7 everyday services within a 15-minute walk.

- 7.8% of dwellings are vacant (2023 census).

- Population change 2016–2022: +2.4%.

- 290,585 hectares of natural, agricultural and forest land consumed between 2011 and 2025.

Four types of territory

The entry map shows the type of territory (inter-municipality, or municipality where the local plan is municipal). By département (96 départements), share of households and number of départements:

- Tight market (vacancy under 5%): 2% of households, 3 départements. Levers: mobilise under-occupation, convert vacant offices and premises, contract private dwellings at social rents.

- Balanced market (vacancy 5 to 8%): 62% of households, 38 départements. Levers: prevent long-term vacancy and retrofit the old stock.

- Sprawling while emptying (vacancy 8% or more, land consumed for housing, population flat or falling): 15% of households, 28 départements. Levers: freeze extensions, reclaim the centre and brownfields.

- Vacancy to reabsorb (vacancy 8% or more, population growing): 21% of households, 27 départements. Levers: bring vacant dwellings back before any new build.

The 15 départements with the highest share of vacant dwellings (2023 census)

Creuse 15.3%; Allier 14.4%; Nièvre 13.5%; Indre 12.8%; Cher 12.2%; Haute-Loire 11.8%; Haute-Marne 11.7%; Yonne 11.6%; Meuse 11.4%; Lot-et-Garonne 11.3%; Ardennes 11.3%; Cantal 11.2%; Aveyron 11.1%; Corrèze 10.9%; Puy-de-Dôme 10.5%.

Other maps in the Atlas

The interactive Atlas also maps, by département and inter-municipality: social housing and the SRU law, the building stock, the night-time urban heat island, carbon and mobility, living conditions in the home (winter cold, cramped and overcrowded dwellings), access to everyday services and green space, and housing affordability. These maps need the interactive version.

Sources and confidentiality

Fichiers fonciers 2021 and 2025 (DGFiP, processed by Cerema); 2023 census, Filosofi 2021 and 2025 permanent facilities database (INSEE); ADMIN EXPRESS 2026 and Géoportail de l'urbanisme (IGN); Cartofriches and land consumption (Cerema); SDES; ANIL 2025 rents; OpenStreetMap and transport.data.gouv.fr. Travel times were computed on the Jean Zay supercomputer (IDRIS). On 21 November 2026, walking, cycling, car and green-space access are computed in every region; public transport in Aquitaine, Bourgogne and Corsica only.

The Atlas contains no personal data. Any value based on fewer than 11 dwellings or premises is hidden. Full method and limits: section "Sources, method and limits" of the interactive Atlas.
