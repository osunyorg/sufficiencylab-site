WHAT THE ATLAS SHOWS
Two diagnoses for each territory in France:
1. What already exists and is not used: vacant and under-occupied dwellings, empty premises, brownfields, land.
2. Whether people can live decently in our territories, estimated with the habitability index: cold, cramped space, access to services and green space, housing cost burden. The index runs from 0 (worst) to 100 (best); the Atlas uses 50 as the floor.

KEY FIGURES FOR FRANCE
- 1,493,721 dwellings vacant for 2 years or more.
- 13.9 million under-occupied main residences.
- 24% of dwellings below the habitability floor of 50.
- 26% of households have the 7 everyday services within a 15-minute walk.
- 7.8% of dwellings vacant (2023 census).
- Population change 2016-2022: +2.4%.
- 290,585 hectares of natural, agricultural and forest land consumed between 2011 and 2025.

FOUR TYPES OF TERRITORY
The entry map crosses the two diagnoses, by département or inter-municipality. Vacancy is high from 8% of vacant dwellings; habitability is poor when the share of dwellings below the floor of 50 is above the national average (24%).
- Renovate to bring back into use (vacancy 8% or more, poor habitability): 21% of households, 37 départements. Levers: targeted renovation of run-down vacant dwellings (OPAH-RU, renovation grants) before any new build.
- Improve the existing stock (vacancy below 8%, poor habitability): 14% of households, 10 départements. Levers: thermal renovation, cooler roofs, green space and local services.
- Bring back into use first (vacancy 8% or more, acceptable habitability): 15% of households, 18 départements. Levers: vacancy tax, contacting owners, rental intermediation, vacant public stock first.
- Make better use of the existing stock (vacancy below 8%, acceptable habitability): 50% of households, 31 départements. Levers: home swaps, intergenerational house-sharing, dividing large homes, converting vacant offices.

DÉPARTEMENTS WITH THE HIGHEST SHARE OF VACANT DWELLINGS (2023 CENSUS)
Creuse 15.3%; Allier 14.4%; Nièvre 13.5%; Indre 12.8%; Cher 12.2%; Haute-Loire 11.8%; Haute-Marne 11.7%; Yonne 11.6%; Meuse 11.4%; Lot-et-Garonne 11.3%; Ardennes 11.3%; Cantal 11.2%; Aveyron 11.1%; Corrèze 10.9%; Puy-de-Dôme 10.5%.

OTHER MAPS IN THE ATLAS
By département and inter-municipality: social housing and the SRU law, the building stock, the night-time urban heat island, carbon and mobility, access to everyday services and green space, and the housing-market typology. These maps need the interactive version.

SOURCES AND CONFIDENTIALITY
Fichiers fonciers 2021 and 2025 (DGFiP, processed by Cerema); 2023 census, Filosofi 2021 and 2025 permanent facilities database (INSEE); ADMIN EXPRESS 2026 and Géoportail de l'urbanisme (IGN); Cartofriches and land consumption (Cerema); SDES; ANIL 2025 rents; OpenStreetMap and transport.data.gouv.fr. Travel times computed on the Jean Zay supercomputer (IDRIS). On 21 November 2026, walking, cycling, car and green-space access are computed in every region; public transport in Aquitaine, Bourgogne and Corsica only. The Atlas contains no personal data; any value based on fewer than 11 dwellings or premises is hidden.
