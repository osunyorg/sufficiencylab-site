PhD Position (3-Year Doctoral Contract) on Sufficiency-Driven Business Models for Health Systems

The doctoral project will be carried out under the co-supervision of Yamina Saheb, WSL's Executive Director, and Sihem Jouini, Associate Professor at HEC Paris, Director of the Innovation and design Project Major and the Executive MBA Major "Innovate as an Entrepreneur" Project context The 3S project ( Systèmes de Santé Sobres ) is a flagship initiative within the World Sufficiency Lab's strategy to co-produce actionable scientific knowledge with societal stakeholders on critical sufficiency policy challenges.
The doctoral candidate will design and implement an innovative action-research programme in partnership with actors across the French health system, with the objective of supporting the metamorphosis of healthcare delivery and governance models to meet emerging health needs under conditions of demographic ageing, ecological degradation, and resource constraints.
To our knowledge, no current research initiative is specifically dedicated to anticipating and preparing the French health system for the profound systemic transformations that these intersecting challenges are expected to generate.
Combining insights from public health, sustainability science, economics, and systems analysis, the project will employ a robust mixed-methods approach, including systematic literature reviews, systems modelling, focus groups, and machine learning techniques. Working closely with sector stakeholders, it will co-produce evidence-based policy pathways and governance innovations to enhance the resilience, sustainability, and adaptive capacity of the French health system.  
Research
objectives
The primary objective of this research is to conduct a forward-looking and participatory inquiry aimed at co-identifying, alongside relevant stakeholders, the transformations required for the French health system to meet future population health needs in the context of demographic ageing and accelerating ecological pressures.
More specifically, the doctoral candidate will investigate the organisational, economic, and governance models capable of ensuring equitable access to high-quality healthcare while strengthening the long-term sustainability and resilience of the health system. Particular attention will be paid to the financing mechanisms, institutional arrangements, workforce requirements, and socio-economic and environmental implications associated with these models.
The research will seek to identify:

Organisational and service-delivery models capable of guaranteeing universal access to high-quality healthcare for all while remaining within, or minimising pressures on, planetary boundaries.
The financing mechanisms, governance arrangements, and health workforce competencies required to support the implementation and scaling of these models.
The socio-economic and environmental impacts of selected models, assessed through quantitative and systems-based modelling approaches.
Evidence-based public policy recommendations capable of facilitating the transition towards more resilient, equitable, and sustainable health systems.

Research
questions
The research will address the following overarching questions
How must the French health system evolve to protect and promote population health, particularly that of older adults, in a context characterised by digital transformation, demographic ageing, and escalating environmental disruption?
What contribution can sufficiency-oriented approaches make to enhancing the resilience, sustainability, and adaptive capacity of the French health system?
What roles might existing health-system actors play in future healthcare systems, and how could their organisational and economic models evolve in response to emerging demographic, environmental, and technological challenges?
Which policy, governance, and financing mechanisms are required to support the transition towards sufficiency-oriented health-system models in France and, more broadly, across Europe?

Methodology
This doctoral project adopts a multidisciplinary and mixed-methods approach, combining quantitative, qualitative, and computational research methods to investigate pathways towards a more sustainable, resilient, and equitable French health system.
The research will begin with a systematic literature review examining the intersections between health systems, sustainability transitions, resilience, and sufficiency. This review will establish the project's conceptual and empirical foundations and identify relevant international experiences and emerging policy innovations.
Building on this evidence base, the project will employ systems-based modelling approaches to assess the socio-economic, health, and environmental implications of selected organisational and service-delivery models. These analyses will enable the evaluation of potential trade-offs, synergies, and long-term impacts associated with alternative health-system pathways.
The quantitative component will be complemented by an extensive programme of stakeholder engagement , including focus groups, semi-structured workshops, and participatory foresight exercises involving key actors across the French health system, such as mutual insurance organisations ( mutuelles ), public authorities, healthcare professionals, civil society organisations, and elected representatives. These activities will support the co-production of knowledge and the identification of feasible and desirable transition pathways.
The project will also leverage machine-learning and artificial intelligence tools to support evidence synthesis, policy mapping, and stakeholder analysis, enabling the systematic processing of large bodies of textual and documentary data.
Prior to the commencement of the doctoral research, the Francophone Assises of Sufficiency will be convened to bring together researchers, policymakers, practitioners, and civil-society actors from France, Belgium, Luxembourg, and Switzerland. These Assises will serve as a platform for knowledge exchange, agenda-setting, and stakeholder mobilisation around key sufficiency challenges, with particular attention to the future of health systems. Their outcomes will contribute to refining the research agenda and strengthening the project's transnational network of partners and collaborators. 
Target
audiences and intended impact The research is designed to engage a broad range of stakeholders involved in shaping the future of the French health system. Its primary audiences include senior officials within national and regional administrations, particularly the Ministries of Health, Labour, Social Affairs, Ecological Transition, and Finance; mutual insurance organisations ( mutuelles ); healthcare providers and professional associations; elected representatives at both national and local levels; and civil-society organisations active in the fields of health and social protection.
By generating robust evidence and fostering stakeholder participation throughout the research process, the project seeks to support strategic decision-making on the future of healthcare provision in a context of demographic, environmental, and economic change. Its ultimate objective is to contribute to the metamorphosis towards a more resilient, equitable, and sustainable health system capable of ensuring universal access to prevention, care, and long-term support while responding effectively to the challenges of the twenty-first century.
Beyond its academic contribution, the project aims to strengthen dialogue between researchers, policymakers, practitioners, and citizens, thereby enhancing the capacity of health system actors to anticipate and manage systemic transformations.  
Deliverables
and timeline The doctoral project will be conducted over a three-year period and will generate a range of academic and policy-oriented outputs.
The expected deliverables include:
An initial progress report submitted six months after the start of the doctoral programme, presenting the conceptual framework, literature review, and detailed research design.
A comprehensive progress report at the end of the second year, summarising preliminary findings and stakeholder engagement activities.
Three peer-reviewed scientific articles submitted to leading international journals in the fields of health policy, sustainability transitions, public administration, or related disciplines.
Policy briefs and stakeholder-oriented outputs designed to support knowledge transfer and inform public debate.
A doctoral dissertation submitted at the end of the third year, ideally structured as a thesis by publication integrating the three scientific articles within a coherent analytical framework.
Governance: The project will be supported by a dedicated governance structure designed to ensure scientific excellence, stakeholder engagement, and policy relevance throughout the doctoral programme.
A steering committee composed of funding organisations, institutional partners, and academic supervisors will meet every four months to review progress, provide strategic guidance, and facilitate the translation of research findings into policy and practice. This forum will ensure that the project's research agenda remains responsive to emerging challenges and stakeholder needs while preserving its scientific independence.
Regular co-production workshops and structured exchanges between the doctoral candidate, project partners, and funders will be organised throughout the research process. These interactions will facilitate knowledge sharing, support the refinement of research questions and analytical frameworks, and promote the dissemination and uptake of findings among key health-system stakeholders.
The governance model is intended to foster a continuous dialogue between science, policy, and practice, thereby maximising both the scientific contribution and the societal impact of the research.

Sufficiency Agora Coordinators (Internship)

About the Initiative
The World Sufficiency Lab (WSL) is launching a network of Sufficiency Agoras as pioneering local spaces for dialogue, experimentation, and collective action around sufficiency. The first pilot initiatives will be established in France, with the aim of developing, testing, and refining a model that can subsequently be replicated across municipalities and regions.
Over the coming years, WSL intends to expand the Sufficiency Agora network to Switzerland and other European countries, including Germany, Belgium, the Netherlands, the UK and Spain, thereby fostering a growing transnational community of practice dedicated to sufficiency, democratic innovation, and socio-ecological transformation.
Conceived as both a physical space and an ongoing programme of activities, each Agora will provide an accessible and welcoming environment in which sufficiency can be explored, debated, and experienced in practice. Through citizen dialogues, deliberative processes, workshops, exhibitions, and public events, the Agoras will bring together residents, local organisations, public authorities, researchers, and practitioners to collectively identify pathways towards more sustainable, equitable, and resilient futures.
To support the implementation of this initiative, WSL is seeking several  Sufficiency Agora Coordinators .

Mission
Under the supervision of the Executive Director of the French Sufficiency Lab and in close collaboration with WSL's Dialogues & Politics team, the Sufficiency Agora Coordinator will lead the local implementation and day-to-day development of pilot Agoras in pioneering municipalities and territories.
The successful candidate will play a central role in transforming the Agora from a conceptual framework into a vibrant community platform for dialogue, learning, experimentation, and action. This will involve building local partnerships, designing and facilitating participatory activities, conducting a territorial metabolism study, and documenting lessons learned to support the future scaling and replication of the model across the World Sufficiency Lab network.
This position offers a unique opportunity to work at the intersection of research, democratic participation, territorial innovation, and ecological transition, while contributing directly to one of WSL's flagship initiatives.

Our activities span research, capacity building, public dialogue, and policy design, and are carried out in partnership with academic institutions, public authorities, civil-society organisations, and private-sector actors across Europe and beyond.

The doctoral project will be carried out under the co-supervision of Yamina Saheb, WSL's Executive Director, and Sihem Jouini, Associate Professor at HEC Paris, Director of the Innovation and design Project Major and the Executive MBA Major "Innovate as an Entrepreneur"

Project context

The 3S project (Systèmes de Santé Sobres) is a flagship initiative within the World Sufficiency Lab's strategy to co-produce actionable scientific knowledge with societal stakeholders on critical sufficiency policy challenges.

The doctoral candidate will design and implement an innovative action-research programme in partnership with actors across the French health system, with the objective of supporting the metamorphosis of healthcare delivery and governance models to meet emerging health needs under conditions of demographic ageing, ecological degradation, and resource constraints.

To our knowledge, no current research initiative is specifically dedicated to anticipating and preparing the French health system for the profound systemic transformations that these intersecting challenges are expected to generate.

Combining insights from public health, sustainability science, economics, and systems analysis, the project will employ a robust mixed-methods approach, including systematic literature reviews, systems modelling, focus groups, and machine learning techniques. Working closely with sector stakeholders, it will co-produce evidence-based policy pathways and governance innovations to enhance the resilience, sustainability, and adaptive capacity of the French health system.  

Research objectives

The primary objective of this research is to conduct a forward-looking and participatory inquiry aimed at co-identifying, alongside relevant stakeholders, the transformations required for the French health system to meet future population health needs in the context of demographic ageing and accelerating ecological pressures.

More specifically, the doctoral candidate will investigate the organisational, economic, and governance models capable of ensuring equitable access to high-quality healthcare while strengthening the long-term sustainability and resilience of the health system. Particular attention will be paid to the financing mechanisms, institutional arrangements, workforce requirements, and socio-economic and environmental implications associated with these models.

The research will seek to identify:

  • Organisational and service-delivery models capable of guaranteeing universal access to high-quality healthcare for all while remaining within, or minimising pressures on, planetary boundaries.
  • The financing mechanisms, governance arrangements, and health workforce competencies required to support the implementation and scaling of these models.
  • The socio-economic and environmental impacts of selected models, assessed through quantitative and systems-based modelling approaches.
  • Evidence-based public policy recommendations capable of facilitating the transition towards more resilient, equitable, and sustainable health systems.

Research questions

The research will address the following overarching questions

  1. How must the French health system evolve to protect and promote population health, particularly that of older adults, in a context characterised by digital transformation, demographic ageing, and escalating environmental disruption?
  2. What contribution can sufficiency-oriented approaches make to enhancing the resilience, sustainability, and adaptive capacity of the French health system?
  3. What roles might existing health-system actors play in future healthcare systems, and how could their organisational and economic models evolve in response to emerging demographic, environmental, and technological challenges?
  4. Which policy, governance, and financing mechanisms are required to support the transition towards sufficiency-oriented health-system models in France and, more broadly, across Europe?

Methodology

This doctoral project adopts a multidisciplinary and mixed-methods approach, combining quantitative, qualitative, and computational research methods to investigate pathways towards a more sustainable, resilient, and equitable French health system.

The research will begin with a systematic literature review examining the intersections between health systems, sustainability transitions, resilience, and sufficiency. This review will establish the project's conceptual and empirical foundations and identify relevant international experiences and emerging policy innovations.

Building on this evidence base, the project will employ systems-based modelling approaches to assess the socio-economic, health, and environmental implications of selected organisational and service-delivery models. These analyses will enable the evaluation of potential trade-offs, synergies, and long-term impacts associated with alternative health-system pathways.

The quantitative component will be complemented by an extensive programme of stakeholder engagement, including focus groups, semi-structured workshops, and participatory foresight exercises involving key actors across the French health system, such as mutual insurance organisations (mutuelles), public authorities, healthcare professionals, civil society organisations, and elected representatives.

These activities will support the co-production of knowledge and the identification of feasible and desirable transition pathways. The project will also leverage machine-learning and artificial intelligence tools to support evidence synthesis, policy mapping, and stakeholder analysis, enabling the systematic processing of large bodies of textual and documentary data.

Prior to the commencement of the doctoral research, the Francophone Assises of Sufficiency will be convened to bring together researchers, policymakers, practitioners, and civil-society actors from France, Belgium, Luxembourg, and Switzerland. These Assises will serve as a platform for knowledge exchange, agenda-setting, and stakeholder mobilisation around key sufficiency challenges, with particular attention to the future of health systems. Their outcomes will contribute to refining the research agenda and strengthening the project's transnational network of partners and collaborators. 

Target audiences and intended impact

The research is designed to engage a broad range of stakeholders involved in shaping the future of the French health system. Its primary audiences include senior officials within national and regional administrations, particularly the Ministries of Health, Labour, Social Affairs, Ecological Transition, and Finance; mutual insurance organisations (mutuelles); healthcare providers and professional associations; elected representatives at both national and local levels; and civil-society organisations active in the fields of health and social protection.

By generating robust evidence and fostering stakeholder participation throughout the research process, the project seeks to support strategic decision-making on the future of healthcare provision in a context of demographic, environmental, and economic change. Its ultimate objective is to contribute to the metamorphosis towards a more resilient, equitable, and sustainable health system capable of ensuring universal access to prevention, care, and long-term support while responding effectively to the challenges of the twenty-first century.

Beyond its academic contribution, the project aims to strengthen dialogue between researchers, policymakers, practitioners, and citizens, thereby enhancing the capacity of health system actors to anticipate and manage systemic transformations.  

Deliverables and timeline

The doctoral project will be conducted over a three-year period and will generate a range of academic and policy-oriented outputs.

The expected deliverables include:

  • An initial progress report submitted six months after the start of the doctoral programme, presenting the conceptual framework, literature review, and detailed research design.
  • A comprehensive progress report at the end of the second year, summarising preliminary findings and stakeholder engagement activities.
  • Three peer-reviewed scientific articles submitted to leading international journals in the fields of health policy, sustainability transitions, public administration, or related disciplines.
  • Policy briefs and stakeholder-oriented outputs designed to support knowledge transfer and inform public debate.
  • A doctoral dissertation submitted at the end of the third year, ideally structured as a thesis by publication integrating the three scientific articles within a coherent analytical framework.

Governance:

The project will be supported by a dedicated governance structure designed to ensure scientific excellence, stakeholder engagement, and policy relevance throughout the doctoral programme.

A steering committee composed of funding organisations, institutional partners, and academic supervisors will meet every four months to review progress, provide strategic guidance, and facilitate the translation of research findings into policy and practice. This forum will ensure that the project's research agenda remains responsive to emerging challenges and stakeholder needs while preserving its scientific independence.

Regular co-production workshops and structured exchanges between the doctoral candidate, project partners, and funders will be organised throughout the research process. These interactions will facilitate knowledge sharing, support the refinement of research questions and analytical frameworks, and promote the dissemination and uptake of findings among key health-system stakeholders.

The governance model is intended to foster a continuous dialogue between science, policy, and practice, thereby maximising both the scientific contribution and the societal impact of the research.

About the Initiative

The World Sufficiency Lab (WSL) is launching a network of Sufficiency Agoras as pioneering local spaces for dialogue, experimentation, and collective action around sufficiency.

The first pilot initiatives will be established in France, with the aim of developing, testing, and refining a model that can subsequently be replicated across municipalities and regions. Over the coming years, WSL intends to expand the Sufficiency Agora network to Switzerland and other European countries, including Germany, Belgium, the Netherlands, the UK and Spain, thereby fostering a growing transnational community of practice dedicated to sufficiency, democratic innovation, and socio-ecological transformation.

Conceived as both a physical space and an ongoing programme of activities, each Agora will provide an accessible and welcoming environment in which sufficiency can be explored, debated, and experienced in practice. Through citizen dialogues, deliberative processes, workshops, exhibitions, and public events, the Agoras will bring together residents, local organisations, public authorities, researchers, and practitioners to collectively identify pathways towards more sustainable, equitable, and resilient futures.

To support the implementation of this initiative, WSL is seeking several Sufficiency Agora Coordinators.

Mission

Under the supervision of the Executive Director of the French Sufficiency Lab and in close collaboration with WSL's Dialogues & Politics team, the Sufficiency Agora Coordinator will lead the local implementation and day-to-day development of pilot Agoras in pioneering municipalities and territories.

The successful candidate will play a central role in transforming the Agora from a conceptual framework into a vibrant community platform for dialogue, learning, experimentation, and action. This will involve building local partnerships, designing and facilitating participatory activities, conducting a territorial metabolism study, and documenting lessons learned to support the future scaling and replication of the model across the World Sufficiency Lab network.

This position offers a unique opportunity to work at the intersection of research, democratic participation, territorial innovation, and ecological transition, while contributing directly to one of WSL's flagship initiatives.

  • A cover letter (of maximum two pages) outlining the candidate's motivation and relevant experience.

Application Deadline: September 4th, 2026

Submission Procedure

Applications should be sent by email to: info@thesufficiencylab.org with the subject line: "Sufficiency Community Manager Application - [Candidate Name]"

Selection Process

Applications will be assessed on the basis of relevant experience, communication and facilitation skills, community-building capabilities, and alignment with the mission of the World Sufficiency Lab.

Shortlisted candidates will be invited to an interview with members of the Executive Team.

The World Sufficiency Lab reserves the right to close the recruitment process before the stated deadline if a suitable candidate is identified.
