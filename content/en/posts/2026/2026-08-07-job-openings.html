Sufficiency Agora Lead
Sufficiency Agora Coordinator
WSL is launching a network of Sufficiency Agoras as pioneering local
spaces for dialogue, experimentation, and collective action on sufficiency. The
first pilot initiatives will be held in France, helping to develop, test, and
refine a model that can be replicated across municipalities and regions. In the
coming months, WSL aims to expand the Sufficiency Agora network to Switzerland
and other European countries, including Germany, the Netherlands, and Spain,
fostering a growing transnational community of practice around sufficiency and
democratic innovation.

Conceived as both a place and a programme, the Agora will serve as a
welcoming community space where sufficiency can be explored, debated, and
experienced in practice. Through citizen dialogues, deliberative processes,
workshops, and public events, it will bring together residents, local
organisations, public authorities, and researchers to collectively explore
pathways towards more sustainable, equitable, and resilient ways of living.

WSL is seeking a Sufficiency Agora Coordinator(s)  to play a pivotal
role in launching and developing this initiative.    Mission:  Under the supervision of the Executive Director of the French Sufficiency Lab and in close collaboration
with WSL's Dialogues & Politics team, the Sufficiency Agora Coordinator will
lead the practical development and day-to-day implementation of the Agoras in pioneering
local authorities.

The successful candidate will transform the Agora from a concept into a
vibrant community space and programme by developing local partnerships,
designing and facilitating participatory activities, conducting a territorial
metabolism study, and documenting lessons learned to support replication across
the World Sufficiency Lab network.

This role offers a unique opportunity to work at the intersection of
research, democratic participation, territorial innovation, and ecological
transition, while making a visible and lasting contribution to one of WSL's
most ambitious initiatives.

PhD on Sufficient Health Systems

The doctoral project will be carried out under the co-supervision of Yamina Saheb, Director of the WSL, and Sihem Jouini, Associate Professor at HEC Paris, Director of the Innovation and design Project Major and the Executive MBA Major "Innovate as an Entrepreneur" Project context The 3S project (Sufficient Health
Systems / Systèmes de Santé Sobres) is part of the WSL's strategy of
co-producing and sharing scientific knowledge with relevant stakeholders on
priority sufficiency-policy issues. The doctoral candidate will develop an innovative
action-research approach with French health system actors [1] to
support the metamorphosis of their business models to the future health needs of
the population, in the context of demographic ageing and the worsening
ecological crisis.

To our knowledge, no current
research project specifically aims to prepare the French health system for the
systemic transformations it will inevitably face. This multidisciplinary
research will mobilise robust scientific tools (systematic literature review,
modelling, focus groups, machine-learning techniques) to co-create, with sector
actors, public-policy proposals facilitating the transformation of the French
health system.

Research
objectives

The principal objective is a
forward-looking exercise aimed at co-identifying, with relevant stakeholders,
the changes required for the French health system to meet the future needs of
the population in the face of the ecological crisis and demographic ageing. The
doctoral candidate will analyse the business models that can guarantee
equitable access to quality healthcare services, their financing, their
socio-economic and environmental impacts, and the actors involved in these new
models.

Specifically,
the doctoral candidate will identify:

Business
models guaranteeing universal access to quality health services while avoiding
or limiting the overshoot of planetary boundaries.

The
necessary financing as well as the training needs of health personnel.

The
socio-economic and environmental impacts of selected models, estimated through
a modelling exercise.

Public-policy
proposals promoting the development of the selected models.

Research
questions

The research
will address the following four questions:


What changes are required for the French health system
to protect the population, and particularly elderly people, in a digitalised
world and an overheating planet?


What
contribution can sufficiency make to the resilience of the French health
system?
What
role could current actors in the health system play in the future?

What
transformations of the business models of the main health-system actors in
France and Europe are possible?
Methodology

The project
mobilises a multidisciplinary approach combining quantitative and qualitative
methods drawn from scientific research:

Systematic literature review on health systems and
sufficiency.

Modelling of the socio-economic and environmental
impacts of selected business models.

Focus groups and workshops with health system actors
(mutuelles, ministries, healthcare practitioners, elected officials).

Machine-learning tools supporting evidence synthesis
and stakeholder analysis.

Ahead of the
doctoral work, Francophone Assises of Sufficiency will be organised to
bring together sufficiency actors from France, Belgium, Luxembourg and
Switzerland and mobilise them on cross-cutting issues, in particular health.

Target
audiences and intended impact

The research
will inform and engage senior staff of administrations and ministries,
particularly those of Health, Labour and Finance, the network of mutuelles,
elected representatives (members of parliament and local officials), medical
practitioners and health organisations. The objective is to engage
decision-makers and sector professionals in a transition toward a more
resilient and sustainable health system, ensuring equitable access to
prevention and care adapted to tomorrow's challenges.

Deliverables
and timeline

The thesis
will run for three years. The doctoral candidate will produce:


An intermediate progress report six months after the
start of the thesis.


An end-of-Year-2 progress report.

Three peer-reviewed scientific articles submitted to
international journals.


The thesis manuscript at the end of Year 3, ideally
structured as a synthesis of the three articles.

Governance: A steering committee bringing together the financial partners, institutional
partners and research partners will meet every four months to associate funders
fully with the governance of the thesis. Co-supervision discussions between the
doctoral candidate and the funders will be organised throughout the thesis to
feed and disseminate the work.

and conditions

already secured funding for the project through its partnership with the
insurance sector and will apply for complementary funding from the ANRT once
the candidate has been selected.

years, with an effective start date no later than September 2026 and an end

with possible missions in France and Europe in connection with the steering
